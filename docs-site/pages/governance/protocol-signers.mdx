# Protocol Signer Policy

The platform-level `MultiSigTreasury` is a separate governance body from the signers of any individual Chama. It protects platform-wide authorities, including the `UpgradeableBeacon` when BeaconProxy deployments are used.

## Signer Composition

The five seats should balance technical capability, operational knowledge, and independence:

1. **Core technical team (1-2 seats)**: a smart-contract architect or CTO who understands upgrades and incident response.
2. **Operations and finance lead (1 seat)**: a CEO, COO, or finance officer responsible for treasury operations.
3. **External trusted parties (1-2 seats)**: independent security advisors, ecosystem grant leads, or trusted community delegates.

The contract enforces five unique, nonzero addresses, 3-of-5 approval, and a mandatory 48-hour delay. It cannot prove that those keys belong to five independent people. Before deployment and after every replacement, record the seat holder, identity verification, conflict-of-interest review, and confirmation that the key is independently controlled.

## Key and Availability Standards

- Use a dedicated hardware wallet or isolated Web3 wallet for every signer.
- Never share a seed phrase, device, or hot wallet between signers.
- Distribute signers across locations and schedules so three approvals can be gathered promptly.
- Maintain a tested recovery procedure without disclosing private material.
- Perform a quarterly signing test and verify a current contact route for every seat.

## Replacement Triggers

Begin replacement when a signer:

- leaves the team or no longer holds the role;
- reports a lost, exposed, inaccessible, or suspected-compromised key; or
- is inactive for more than 30 consecutive days without approved leave and a recorded return date.

Human inactivity and independence are operational checks. The smart contract cannot detect either condition by itself.

## Replacement Flow

1. Record the trigger, affected seat, incident details, and proposed replacement in the governance log.
2. Notify the remaining signers and review the replacement for conflicts of interest and independence.
3. If compromise is suspected, stop sensitive proposals and use the existing multisig-controlled pause or incident process where available. Never use a private-key shortcut.
4. Submit a transaction targeting `MultiSigTreasury` itself that calls `replaceSigner(oldSigner, newSigner)`.
5. Obtain confirmation from 3 of 5 signers and wait the mandatory 48-hour on-chain timelock.
6. Execute the transaction, verify the signer list, rotate affected operational credentials, update the governance log, and run a signing test with the new signer.

If the remaining available signers cannot reach 3-of-5, there is no unilateral recovery path in the contract. Follow the incident-recovery procedure; redeployment may be required.

## Annual Review and Rotation

Review the five seats annually. The contract can enforce a pre-approved annual rotation schedule, but the team must select and verify the replacement and assess real-world independence.

This policy applies to the platform multisig. Each Chama manages its local signers under its own governance rules.
